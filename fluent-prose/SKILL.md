# Fluent Prose

Write in smooth, natural prose with strong sentence-level fluency. The writing should sound natural when read aloud, and each paragraph should feel like one developing idea rather than a collection of disconnected statements.

## Sentence rhythm

Use a deliberate mix of short, medium, and longer complex sentences so the writing has a natural human cadence. Do not let every sentence follow the same length, structure, or rhythm.

Prioritize continuous thought over artificially concise sentences. Avoid unnecessary abrupt stops, excessive sentence splitting, and sequences of statements that read like separate notes rather than one continuous argument.

## Format

Write primarily in complete prose paragraphs. Avoid bullet points, numbered lists, bolded introductory fragments, and isolated sentence fragments unless the requested format specifically requires them.

## Varied syntax

Do not repeatedly rely on simple Subject + Verb + Object constructions. Vary sentence openings by using dependent clauses, prepositional phrases, time markers, contextual phrases, transitions, and occasional inversion where natural.

Avoid repeatedly beginning sentences with the same subject or phrase. If several consecutive sentences begin with constructions such as "AI can," "AI does," "AI helps," or "[Company] is," restructure them by changing the clause order or sentence opening.

Instead of:

> AI automates routine tasks. AI saves employees time. AI helps teams focus.

Prefer something like:

> By automating routine tasks, AI gives employees more time to focus on higher-value work, allowing teams to direct their attention toward problems that actually require judgment.

## Connecting ideas

Connect related thoughts instead of breaking them into several short sentences. When two ideas naturally belong together, combine them using conjunctions, dependent clauses, or transitions.

Avoid fragmented constructions such as:

> To improve performance. The team needs better systems.

Instead, write:

> To improve performance, the team needs better systems.

## Transitions

Use transitions when they genuinely improve flow, particularly when moving between contrasting, causal, or additive ideas. Useful patterns include:

- Contrast: however, while, whereas, although, on the other hand.
- Cause and effect: because, therefore, as a result, consequently, which means that.
- Addition and continuation: furthermore, in addition, while also, not only... but also.

Do not mechanically insert transition words into every sentence. The writing should feel connected without sounding formulaic.

## Matching a mentor text

When a mentor text or writing sample is provided, study its sentence length, rhythm, clause structure, transition style, pacing, and paragraph flow. Match those characteristics closely while preserving the meaning and requirements of the new content. Do not copy distinctive wording from the reference unless explicitly instructed.

## Final check

Before finalizing, silently check the prose for choppiness. If several consecutive sentences are short, disconnected, or structurally repetitive, rewrite the passage so related ideas flow together naturally. Do not mention this check in the response.
